ABOUT ME

As someone who began as a traditional visual artist and gradually moved toward software, digital illustration, and game-based thinking, I consider myself a new media artist because I find it useful as a way of understanding my own transition from physical materials to computational and digital ones. I am drawn to both physical and digital media, but recently the digital workspace has become somewhat of a personal playground. Learning Adobe tools and Procreate felt similar to learning an instrument; I practiced brushes, lighting with multiply layers, and imitating styles from Spider-Verse or Arcane to understand how they were built. Studying software engineering then gave me the realization that images can respond to code, physics, and user input. Game development attracts me a lot because it combines drawing, animation, writing, and systems thinking into one. The questions I want my work to engage are about how identity and emotion live inside digital structures. How does a style created by algorithms shape what a character can feel? I want to create small interactive worlds, playful websites, or games where the mechanics carry meaning. My practice lies in treating technology as a meta material, even though I find that I keep a rather strong connection to hand-drawn art and storytelling. For me, new media art is using technology consciously - I situate myself in the new media as a person using the tools I learn to create engaging and boundary-pushing art and expression.

Through my work, I explore how identity and emotion exist inside digital spaces. I enjoy creating projects that feel immersive while still reflecting my personal themes and hand-made visual aesthetics.

Current interests include digital illustration, creative coding, interactive websites, experimental games, and motion design.
